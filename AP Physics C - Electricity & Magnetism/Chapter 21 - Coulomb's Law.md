# 21.1 Coulomb's Law
- The strength of a particle's electrical interaction with objects around it depends on its **electric charge**, which can be either positive or negative. Particles with the same sign of charge repel each other, and particles with opposite signs of charge attract each other.
- An object with equal amounts of the two kinds of charge is electrically neutral, whereas one with an imbalance is electrically charged and has an excess charge
- **Conductors** are materials in which a significant number of electrons are free to move. The charged particles in **nonconductors (insulators)** are not free to move.
- **Electric current _i_** is the rate at which charge passes a point: $$i = \frac{dq}{dt}$$
	- 1 C = (1 A)(1 s)
- **Coulomb's Law** describes the electrostatic force between 2 charged particles. If the particles have charges $q_1$ and $q_2$ respectively, are separated by distance _r_, and are at rest (or moving only slowly) relative to each other, then the magnitude of the force acting on each due to the other is given by $$\vec{F} = k\frac{q_1q_2}{r^2}\hat{r} \quad \text{and} \quad F = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{|q_1||q_2|}{r^2}$$
	- $\varepsilon_0 = 8.85 \times 10^{-12}$ C\^2 / N m\^2 
	- $1/4\pi \varepsilon_0$ often replaced by Coulomb (electrostatic) constant $k = 8.99 \times 10^9$ N m\^2 / C\^2 
- The electrostatic force vector acting on a charged particle due to a second charged particle is either directly toward the second particle (opp. signs of charge) or directly away from it (same sign)
- If multiple electrostatic forces act on a particle, the net force is the vector sum of the individual forces
- Shell Theorem
	- Shell Theorem 1: A charged particle outside a shell with charge uniformly distributed on its surface is attracted or repelled as if the shell's charge were concentrated as a particle at its center
	- Shell Theorem 2: "" has no net force acing on it due to the shell
- Charge on a conducting spherical shell spreads uniformly over the external surface

# 21.2 Charge is Quantized
- Electric charge is quantized
- The charge of a particle can be written as _ne_, where _n_ is a pos/neg integer and _e_ is the elementary charge, the magnitude of the charge of the electron and proton ($\approx 1.602 \times 10^{-19}$ C)

# 21.3 Charge is Conserved
- The net electric charge of any isolated system is always conserved.
- If 2 charged particles undergo an annihilation process, they have opp. signs of charge
- If 2 charged particles appear as a result of a pair production process, they have opp. signs of charge

# 36.1 Single-Slit Diffraction
- When waves encounter an edge, obstacle, or qperture the sie of which is comperable to the wavelength of the waves, those wavs spread out as they travel and, as a result, undergo interference. This type of interference is called diffraction.
- Waves passing through a long narrow slit of width $a$ produce, on a viewing screen, a single-slit diffraction pattern that includes a central maximum (bright fringe) and other maxima. They are separated by minima that are located relative to the centra axis by angles $\theta$: $$a \sin \theta = m\lambda$$
- The maxima are located approx. halfway between minima
# 36.2 Intensity in Single-Slit Diffraction
- The intensity of the diffraction pattern at any given angle $\theta$ is $$I(\theta)=I_m(\frac{\sin\alpha}{\alpha})^2$$where $I_m$ is the intensity at the center of the pattern and $$\alpha = \frac{\pi a}{\lambda}\sin \theta$$
# 36.3 Diffraction by a Circular Aperture
- Diffraction by a circular aperture or lens with diameter $d$ produces a central maximum and concentric maxima and minima, with the first minimum at an angle $\theta$ given by $$\sin \theta = 1.22 \frac{\lambda}{d}$$
- Rayleigh's criteron suggests that two objects are on the verge of resolvability if the central diffraction minimum of one is at th first minimum of the other. Their angular separation can then be no less than $$\theta_R = 1.22 \frac{\lambda}{d}$$in which $d$ is the diameter of the aperture through which the light passes. 
- Lens images are diffraction patterns
	- Resolve: distinguish two distant point objects whose angular separtion is small
	- Pointillism-- when you change the distance, colors shift in subtle ways-- depends on whether you can resolve the colored dots
# 36.4 Diffraction by a Double Slit
- Waves passing through two slits produce a combination of double-slit interference and diffraction by each slit
- For identical slits with width $a$ and center-to-center separation $d$, the intensity in the pattern varies with the angle $\theta$ from the central axis as $$I = I_m(\cos^2\beta)(\frac{\sin \alpha}{\alpha}^2)$$where $I_m$ is the intensity at the center of the patter, $$\beta = \frac{\pi d}{\lambda}\sin \theta$$$$\beta = \frac{\pi a}{\lambda}\sin \theta$$
- generally, 
	- interference-- combining waves originate from small number of elementary coherent sources (double-slit experiment, $a \ll \lambda$)
	- diffraction-- combining waves originate in a single wavefront (single-slit experiment)
# 36.5 Diffraction Gratings
- A diffraction grating is a series of "slits" used to separate an incident wave into its component wavelengths by separating and displaying their diffraction maxima. Diffraction by $N$ slits results in maxima lines at angles $\theta$ such that $$d \sin \theta = m\lambda$$
- Many slits
	- Maxima are very narrow (now called lines)
	- Separated by relatively wide dark regions
- Applications
	- Grating Spectroscope!
		- Collimator
		- Hydrogen Lamp-- white, but when looked at through a spectroscope in several orders there are four colors corresponding to the visible wavelengths (emission lines)
	- Optically Variable Graphics; produces image via diffraction of diffuse light by gratings embedded in the device
		- very difficult to design and thus counterfeit
# 36.6 Gratings: Dispersion and Resolving Power
- The dispersion $D$ of a diffraction grating is a measure of the angular separation $\Delta \theta$ of the lines it produces for two wavelengths differing by $\Delta \lambda$. For order number _m_, at angle $\theta$ $$D = \frac{\Delta \theta}{\Delta \lambda}=\frac{m}{d \cos \theta}$$
	- higher dispersion --> greater angular separation between lines
- The rsolving power $R$ of a diffraction grating is a measure of its ability to make the emission lines of two close wavelengths distinguishable. For two wavelengths different by $\Delta \lambda$ and with an average value of $\lambda_{avg}$, the resolving power is given by $$R = \frac{\lambda_avg}{\Delta \theta} = Nm$$
	- higher resolving powier --> narrower lines, lines are more distinguishable

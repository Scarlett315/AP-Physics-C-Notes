# 35.1 Interference
- The 3D transmission of waves may often by predicted by Huygen's Principle
	- All points on a wavefront serve as point sources of spherical secondary wavelets. After a time, the new position of the wavefront will be that of a surface tangent to these secondary wavelets.
- The law of refraction can be derived from Huygens' principle
- The wavelength of light $\lambda_n$ of light in a medium depends on the IR $n$ of the medium: $$\lambda_n=\frac{\lambda}{n}$$in which $\lambda$ is the wavelength of the light in vacuum. 
- Because of this dependency, the phase difference between two waves can change if they pass through different materials with different IRs
- Interference and rainbows

# 35.2 Young's Interference Experiment
- In Young's interference experiment, light passing through a single slit falls on two slits in a screen. The light leaving these slits flares out (by diffraction) and interference occurs in the region beyond the screen. A fringe pattern, due to the interference, forms on a viewing screen.
- Conditions for max/min intensity $$d \sin \theta = m \lambda$$ $$d \sin \theta = (m + \frac{1}{2}\lambda)$$where $\theta$ is the angle the light path makes with a central axis and $d$ is the slit separation
- Proved that light is a wave
- **diffraction**: If a wave encounters a barrier that has an opening of dimensions similar to the wavelength, the part of the wave that passes through the opening will flare out (diffract) into the region beyond the barrier
# 35.3 Interference and Double-Slit Intensity
- If two light waves that meet at a point are to interfere perceptibly, the phase diference between them must remain constant with time; that is, the waves must be coherent. When two coherent waves meet, the resulting intensity may be found using phasors. 

# 35.4 Interference from Thin Films
- When light is incident on a thin transparent film, the light waves reflected from the front an dback surfaces interfere.
- If a film is sandwiched between media other than air, these equations for bright and dark films may be interchanged, depending on the relatives indexes of refraction.
- If the light incident at an interface between media with different indexes of refraction is in the medium with the smaller index of refraction, the reflection causes a phase change of $\pi$ rad, or half a wavelength, in the reflected wave. Otherwise, theres is no phase change due to the reflection. Refraction causes no phase shift.

# 35.5 Michelson's Interferometer
- A light wave is split into two beams that then recombine after traveling along different paths
- The interference pattern they produce depends on the difference in the lengths of those paths and the indexes of refraction along the paths

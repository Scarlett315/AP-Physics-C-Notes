# 34.1 Images and Plane Mirrors
- An image is a reproduction of an object via light. If the image can form on a surface, it is a real image and can exist even if no observer is present. If the image requires the visual system of an observer, it is a virtual image.
	- i.e. virtual images appear to be behind the mirror, real images can be formed on a surface
	- Mirages are a type of virtual image caused by light bending since heat changes refractive index and the road heats up the air
- A plane (flat) mirror can form a virtual image of a light source (said to be the object) by redirecting light rays emerging from the source. The image can be seen where backward extensions of reflected rays pass through one another. The object's distance $p$ from the mirror is related to the (apparent) image distance $i$ by $$i = -p$$Object distance is a positive quantity. Image distance $i$ for a virtual image is a negative quantity.

# 34.2 Spherical Mirrors
- A spherical mirror is in the shape of a small section of a spherical surface and can
	- concave: the radius of curvature $r$ is a pos. quantity
	- convex: r is a negative quantity
	- plane: flate, r is infinite
- if parallel rays are sent into a spherical concave mirror || to the central axis, the reflected rays pass through a common point (a real focus $F$) at a distance $f$, a positive quantity from the mirror. If they are sent toward a (spherica l) convex mirror, backward extensions of the reflected rays pass through a common point $F$ (virtual focus) at a distance $f$ (negative quantity) from the mirror
- A concave mirror can form a real image (if the object is outside the focal point) or a virtual image (if the object is inside the focal point)
- A convex mirror can form only a virtual image
- The mirror equation relates an object distance $p$, the mirror's focal length $f$ and radius of curvature $r$, and the image distance $i$: $$\frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{i} = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{2}{r}$$
- The magnitude of the lateral magnification $m$ of an object is the ratio of the image height $h'$ to object height $h$, $$|m|=\frac{h'}{h}$$and is related to the object distance $p$ and image distance $i$ by $$m = \frac{-i}{p}$$
- Forming a spherical mirror from a plane

		2. field of view is smaller
		3. $i$ is greater
		4. height of the image is now greater
	- Then to convex

# 34.3 Spherical Refracting Surfaces
- A single spherical surface that refracts light can form an image
- The object distance $p$, the image distance $i$, and the radius of curvature $r$ of the surface are related by $$\frac{n_1}{p} + \frac{n_2}{i} = \frac{n_2 - n_1}{r}$$where $n_1$ is the IR of the material where the object is located and $n_2$ is the IR on the other side of the surface
- If the surface faced by the object is convex, $r$ is positive, and if it is concave $r$ is negative
- Images on the object's side of the surface are virtual, images on the opposite side are real
# 34.4 Thin Lenses
- if || rays are sent through a converging lens | to the central axis, the refracted rays pass through a common point (real focus $F$) at a distance $f$ (pos.) from the lens.
- If they are sent through a diverging lens, backward extensions of the refracted rays pass through a common point (virtual focus $F$) at a focal distance $f$ (neg.) from the lens
- A converging lens can form a real image (object outside focal point) or virtual image (object inside focal point)
- A diverging lens can only form a virtual image
- For an object in front of a lens, object distance $p$ and image idstance $i$ are related to the lens' focal length $f$, IR $n$, and radii of curvature $r_1$ and $r_2$ by $$\frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{i} = \frac{1}{f} = (n-1)(\frac{1}{r_1}-\frac{1}{r_2})$$
	- (second part is called lens maker's equation)
- Magnification $$|m|=\frac{h'}{h}$$ $$m = \frac{-i}{p}$$
- For a system of lenses with a common central axis, the image produced by the first lens acts as the objects as the object for the second lens, and so on, and the overal magnification is the product of the individual magnifications

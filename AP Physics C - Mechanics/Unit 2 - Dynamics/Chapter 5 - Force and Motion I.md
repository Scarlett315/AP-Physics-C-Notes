# 5.1 Newton's First and Second Laws
- The velocity of an object can change (acceleration) when the object is acted on by one or more forces (pushes or pulls) from other objects
	- Newtonian mechanics relate accelerations and forces
- Forces are vector quantities:
	-  Magnitude defined in terms of the accel. they wold give the standard kilogram (Force that accelerates standard by 1 m/s^2 = 1N)
	- Direction of the force is the direction of the acceleration
	- Forces are combined using the rules of vector algebra
- **Newton's First Law**: If there is no net force on a body ($\vec{F}_{net} = 0$), the body remains at rest if it is initially at rest or moves in a straight line at constant speed if it is in motion
- Inertial Reference Frame: Reference frames in which Newtonian mechanics hold
	- "Fictitious" / nonexistent forces can appear if using non-inertial reference frames
- The mass of a body is the characteristic of the body that relates the body's acceleration to the net force causing the acceleration; scalar quantity
- **Newton's Second Law**: the net force on a body is equal to the product of the body's mass and it's acceleration, i.e. $$\vec{F}_{net}=m\vec{a}$$
	- Component vers. : $F_{net, x} = ma_x$ ; $F_{net, y} = ma_y$ ; $F_{net, z} = ma_z$ 
	- In SI Units, $\large 1\text{N} = 1\frac{kg \times m}{s^2}$
- **Free-Body Diagram**: stripped-down diagram in which only one body is considered, represented by either a sketch or dot; external forces are drawn, coordinate system is superimposed, oriented so as to simplify the solution
- Only external forces on an object can cause it to accelerate

- **Principle of Superposition for Forces**: A single force that has the same magnitude and direction as the calculated net force would have the same effect as all the individual forces
- Acceleration and mass are inversely proportional: $$\frac{m_x}{m_0}=\frac{a_0}{a_x}$$

# 5.2 Some Particular Forces
- A **gravitational force $\vec{F}_g$** on a body is a pull by another body; usually Earth or some other astronomical body
	- For Earth, the force is directed down toward the ground, which is assumed to be an inertial reference frame.
	- With that assumption, the magnitude is $F_g = m_g$, where _m_ is the body's mass and _g_ is the magnitude of the free-fall acceleration
- **Weight** of a body is the. magnitude of the upward force needed to balance the gravitational force on the body; weight related to mass by $W = mg$ 
- A **normal force $\vec{F}_N$** is the force on a body from a surface against which the body presses. The normal force is always perpendicular to the surface.
- A **frictional force $\vec{f}$** is the force on a body when the body slides or attempts to slide along a surface. The force is always parallel to the surface and directed so as to oppose the sliding. On a frictionless surface, the frictional force is negligible.
- When a cord is under **tension**, each end of the cord pulls on a body. The pull is directed along the cord, away from the point of attachment to the body. For a massless cord, the pulls at both end of the cord have the same magnitude _T_, even if the cord runs around a massless, frictionless pulley

# 5.3 Applying Newton's Laws
- **Newton's Third Law**: If a force $\vec{F}_{BC}$ acts on body _B_ due to body _C_, then there is a force $\vec{F}_{CB}$ on body _C_ due to body _B_: $$\vec{F}_{BC} = -\vec{F}_{CB}$$
	- Forces are equal in magnitude by opposite in direction
	- **Third-Law Force Pair**: The forces between two interacting bodies

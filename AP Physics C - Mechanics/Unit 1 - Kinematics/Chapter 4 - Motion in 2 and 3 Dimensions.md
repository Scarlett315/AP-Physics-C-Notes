# Uniform Circular Motion

>[!abstract]
>- If a particle travels along a circle or circular arc of radius _r_ at constant speed _v_, it is said to be in uniform circular motion and has an acceleration $$\vec{a} = \frac{v^2}{r}$$
>- The direction of $\vec{a}$ is said to be centripetal; toward the center of the circle/circular arc
>- T (period of revolution): the time for the particle to complete a circle, calculated by $$T = \frac{2 \pi r}{v}$$

- **Uniform Circular Motion**: a particle traveling around a circle or circular arc at constant speed
	- The particle is accelerating b/c of a change in direction
	- Velocity is always directed tangent to the circle in the direction of motion
	- Acceleration is directed radially inward (**centripetal (center-seeking) acceleration**) 
	- $\vec{a} = \frac{v^2}{r}$
- Period of Revolution (T, measured in s): the time for a particle to go around aclosed path exactly once
	- $T = \frac{2 \pi r}{v}$

# Relative Motion in 1-D
>[!abstract]
>- When two frames of reference _A_ and _B_ are moving relative to each other at constant velocity, the velocity of a particle _P_ as measured by an observer in frame _A_ usually differs from that measured from frame _B_. The two measured velocities are related by $$\vec v_{PA} = \vec v_{PB} + \vec v_{BA'}$$
>	- where $\vec v_{BA}$ is the velocity of _B_ with respect to _A_
>- Both observers measure the same acceleration for the particle ($\vec a_{PA} + \vec v_{PB}$)

### Displacement
$$\vec x_{PA} = \vec x_{PB} + \vec x_{BA'}$$
- The coordinate $x_{PA}$ of _P_ as measured by _A_ _is equal to_ the coordinate $x_{PB}$ of _P_ as measured by _B_ _plus_ the coordinate $x_{BA}$ of _B_ as measured by _A_ 
### Velocity
$$v_{PA} = \frac{d}{dt}(x_{PA}) = \frac{d}{dt}(x_{PB}) + \frac{d}{dt}(x_{BA})$$
$$\vec v_{PA} = \vec v_{PB} + \vec v_{BA'}$$
### Acceleration
$$a_{PA} = \frac{d}{dt}(v_{PA}) = \frac{d}{dt}(v_{PB}) + \frac{d}{dt}(v_{BA})$$
- $v_{BA}$ is constant & derivative is 0; therefore:
$$\vec a_{PA} + \vec a_{PB}$$
